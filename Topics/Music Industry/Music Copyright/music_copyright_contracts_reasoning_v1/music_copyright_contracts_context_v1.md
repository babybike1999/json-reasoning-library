# Music Copyright Contracts Reasoning Library — compact context

## Purpose and limits

This library helps an assistant prepare rights maps, review clauses, identify missing facts, organize chain-of-title evidence, prepare negotiation questions, and brief qualified counsel for independent music projects. It is an issue-spotting tool. It is not an automatic legal opinion, a ready-to-sign contract set, or a release clearance. Never conclude “safe to sign,” “fully protected,” “release cleared,” or “no infringement risk” from these records.

No particular collaborators, contributions, ownership percentages, fees, royalties, approvals, or agreement facts were supplied. Do not fill them in. Do not insert an “industry-standard” term.

## Four layers: keep each visible

1. **Source:** What the uploaded edition says, including its examples, caveats, and cited authorities.
2. **Extraction:** Whether the words were recovered and visually checked. The source is scanned; a usable embedded text layer is present on most pages. That layer is a working transcription, not authoritative wording.
3. **Application:** A question or workflow that may help review a music project. Clearly label application extensions; do not present them as the book’s doctrine.
4. **Current law:** What current authority was checked for a specific proposition and date. “High source fidelity” means faithful to the book; it does not mean current, universal, enforceable, or applicable to project facts.

The book is *Music Copyright Law* by David J. Moser and Cheryl L. Slay, Course Technology PTR/Cengage, copyright 2012. The authors say the legal discussion was current as of mid-2011. The scanned PDF contains 324 pages. Numbered book pages 1–304 are PDF pages 19–322 (PDF page minus 18). Front matter PDF pages 5–18 uses Roman pages III–XVI. ## Main rights map

Always identify each asset separately:

- **Musical composition:** music and accompanying lyrics.
- **Sound recording/master:** the particular fixed sounds in a recording.
- **Version/deliverable:** alternate mixes, live recordings, audiovisual files, stems, session files, artwork, or metadata may raise separate scope questions.

The current U.S. statute separately lists musical works and sound recordings; sound-recording rights have their own scope and limitations. This does not tell you who owns either asset or whether a particular use needs permission. Do not infer authorship or ownership from “producer,” “arranger,” “session musician,” “engineer,” “studio,” payment, credit, attendance, registration, or possession of files alone. File delivery and copyright permission are separate obligations.

Keep these commercial/legal categories distinct: composition ownership; master ownership; a transfer/assignment; exclusive or nonexclusive permission; administration; collection; publisher share; collection share; a contractual royalty; cash service fees; and credit. A percentage without a defined base is not interpretable. Ask whether it concerns ownership, gross receipts, net receipts, recoupment, a publisher share, or a contract royalty base.

## Review sequence

Purpose → works and versions → parties and authority → contributions → claimed rights → supporting documents → grants and reservations → payment and approvals → conflicting clauses → unresolved facts and law → recommended next step.

For every contract clause, capture the clause locator, plain-language effect, asset/right affected, concern, supporting evidence, source/extraction status, current-law status and jurisdiction, one clarification question tied to an action, priority, and dependency on other clauses. Keep commercial preference separate from the legal framework. Contract wording proves what the text says; it does not prove that the clause is valid or every signer can grant the rights.

## High-consequence gates

- **Work made for hire:** identify the employee/scope route or qualifying commissioned category and the express signed writing. The current statute has two distinct routes. Do not assume a contractor label, clause heading, or payment decides status. Analyze any fallback assignment separately, including what rights and works it covers and the signed-writing question.
- **Joint authorship/splits:** collect each person’s expression, timing, intent to merge, communications, and documents. Separate legal authorship from negotiated splits, credits, services, and royalty participation. The book’s equal-share/licensing discussion is source-era commentary; full co-owner defaults need controlling case/jurisdiction review.
- **Composition/master:** check terms for accidental reach to masters, production files, branding, artwork, other songs, or unrelated rights in a composition-only agreement. A license or clearance for one asset does not establish scope for another.
- **Samples/interpolations/adaptations/audiovisual uses:** identify the actual material and version. Ask about both composition and recording, the source owner, use, territory, term, approvals, and third-party conditions. Do not use a safe-seconds/notes rule. Fair use is fact-specific; no outcome or sampling-case survey is supplied.
- **Registration/remedies:** separate protection, ownership, registration procedure, suit prerequisite, and eligibility for specific remedies. Collect publication, effective registration, preregistration, work-origin, and first-infringement dates. Do not calculate a live deadline or guarantee an award.
- **Duration/territory/digital licensing:** the book’s duration, pre-1972 recording, rate, PRO, online-platform, and enforcement examples may be old or jurisdiction-bound. Verify the current authority and facts for each territory and use.

## Reversible next steps

Useful steps include making a version-level asset schedule; collecting signed agreements and amendments; preserving dated demos, session notes, stems, invoices, and emails; creating a contribution/evidence table; asking a grantor to confirm authority; comparing conflicting defined terms; creating a clearance log; requesting a corrected split/credit/metadata entry; or pausing the affected pitch/release while a material permission or approval remains unresolved.

An invoice, split sheet, registration, email, studio receipt, credit, or distribution entry establishes only what that item actually says in context. Keep chain-of-title status unresolved when a material link lacks support.

## Distinguishing evidence, contract text, law, and preference

For a review, keep four separate notes before interpreting a clause:

- **Facts:** what was created, by whom, when, and what records support that account.
- **Contract text:** the words in the proposed or executed document, its defined terms, signatures, amendments, and cross-references.
- **Legal framework:** work category, jurisdiction, applicable statutory route, controlling cases, exceptions, and procedural conditions.
- **Commercial preference:** the result the parties want to negotiate, including acceptable alternatives and tradeoffs.

A contract can be clear about what it says while leaving authority or enforceability unresolved. A registration, split sheet, email, invoice, receipt, or metadata field has evidentiary value limited to its actual content; none automatically closes a chain-of-title gap. A source locator tells where a book passage appears. An authority reference tells what current external material was checked. Do not use either reference type as a substitute for a missing agreement or project fact.

## Synthetic case use

The eight synthetic cases are diagnostic drills, not examples of actual clients or legal precedents. They cover a paid session participant without an agreement, a producer with both master and composition claims, a broad composition grant, a license mistaken as covering a second asset, unresolved co-owner authority for an exclusive grant, conflicting royalty bases, missing registration dates, and unreadable scan language. A case passes only when the assistant asks for the missing evidence, withholds unsupported legal outcomes, and proposes a useful reversible next step. The unreadable-negation case must remain blocked until the original wording can be inspected.

## Status language

Use **document present** only when the relevant document was actually supplied and reviewed. Use **claim asserted/support pending** when a party states a right but evidence is missing. Use **fact unresolved** when the account or date is incomplete; **conflicting documents** when records disagree; **source-only explanation** when the book supports a paraphrase but no current law was checked; **current verification pending** when the proposition still needs authority research; **permission/approval open** when a required grant or consent is not documented; and **counsel review needed** when controlling law, consequences, or drafting calls for qualified advice. Do not compress several of these states into “low confidence.”
